# How to Add a Stopover to a Flight for Free

A number of major airlines let you turn a standard connecting flight into an extended stay in their hub city — sometimes for several days — at no extra cost beyond your original fare. This is genuinely one of the better-value tricks in flight planning, and it's surprisingly underused simply because it isn't always obvious it's an option.

This guide covers how these free stopover programmes actually work, which airlines currently offer them, and exactly how to book one.

---

## What a Free Stopover Actually Is

Ordinarily, when you connect through an airline's hub en route to your final destination, you pass through with a standard layover — typically a few hours. A **stopover programme** allows you to extend that layover into a longer stay, sometimes up to several days, without paying more than your original one-way or return fare would have cost anyway.

The logic from the airline's side is straightforward: it encourages travellers to route through their specific hub rather than a competitor's, and it showcases their home city as an extra reason to choose that airline. For the traveller, it's effectively a free extra destination bolted onto a flight you were taking anyway.

---

## Which Airlines Currently Offer This

Several airlines have run stopover programmes in recent years, though the specific details, minimum and maximum stay lengths, and eligible routes change over time, so always verify current terms directly with the airline before planning around one. As a general pattern, look for:

**Gulf carriers connecting through their home hub** — some allow stopovers of several days in their hub city, occasionally including partner hotel discounts or city tour add-ons as part of the programme.

**Scandinavian and Icelandic carriers on North Atlantic routes** — a well-known example allows a stopover of several days in Reykjavik for travellers connecting between Europe and North America, effectively adding an Iceland trip to a transatlantic journey at no extra flight cost.

**Some Asian carriers connecting through their hub cities** — occasionally offering free or heavily discounted extended stopovers as part of a promotional or standing programme.

**Turkish Airlines and similar carriers positioned as a bridge between continents** — have at various points offered free hotel stays or stopover programmes for longer connections through Istanbul.

Because these programmes come and go, and their specific terms vary considerably, the most reliable approach is searching the specific airline's own website for "stopover programme" alongside your planned hub city, rather than assuming a programme you've heard of is still running on the same terms.

## How to Actually Book One

**Step 1: Identify a route that naturally connects through a stopover-friendly hub.** If you're already planning to fly, for example, from Europe to North America, or from Europe to Asia via a Gulf hub, check whether that airline's specific hub offers a stopover programme before booking.

**Step 2: Search directly on the airline's own website, not a third-party search engine.** Stopover options are typically only available or clearly presented when booking directly through the airline, since they involve a specific booking flow (sometimes a dedicated "stopover" search tool) that general search engines don't always replicate.

**Step 3: Select your stopover duration during booking.** Most programmes let you choose the length of your stopover, up to a maximum set by the airline, directly during the booking process — this is usually presented as a step within the standard booking flow rather than a separate add-on you configure afterward.

**Step 4: Check whether accommodation or extras are included, and whether you need to book those separately.** Some programmes bundle a discounted or free hotel stay as part of the deal; others only cover the flight stopover itself, leaving you to arrange your own accommodation in the city.

**Step 5: Confirm your visa requirements for the stopover country specifically.** Even with an airline-sponsored stopover programme, standard entry requirements typically still apply — some countries offer visa-free or simplified entry for stopover programme participants specifically, but this isn't universal, so check the specific rule for your nationality and the stopover country.

## Is This the Same as Just Booking a Multi-City Flight Myself?

Not quite, though the end result — spending meaningful time in an extra city as part of a longer journey — is similar to what you could achieve with a manually planned open-jaw or multi-city itinerary covered elsewhere. The key differences:

**A formal stopover programme is typically priced the same as the underlying point-to-point fare** — you're not paying extra for the additional city, whereas a self-built multi-city itinerary adding an extra destination usually does add some cost, even if modest.

**Formal stopover programmes are limited to that specific airline's hub city**, whereas building your own multi-city or open-jaw route gives you complete freedom over which cities to include, at the cost of it not being free in the same way.

**A stopover programme keeps your itinerary on a single, protected ticket**, since it's just an extended version of the connection you were already booked for — a self-built multi-city itinerary may or may not be similarly protected, depending on how you structure it.

If your ideal extra destination happens to be one of the airlines' stopover hub cities, the formal programme is usually the better-value option. If it isn't, building your own multi-city route gives you the flexibility to choose freely, even though it typically isn't free in the same specific sense.

## Practical Considerations Before Booking a Stopover

**Check how the stopover affects your total journey time and any onward connections.** A multi-day stopover obviously extends your total trip length considerably — factor this into any other bookings (accommodation, activities, work commitments) tied to your original destination dates.

**Understand how baggage is handled during a multi-day stopover.** Some programmes allow your checked baggage to be held at the airport for a shorter layover-style stopover, but for a multi-day stay, you'll typically need to collect your bags and bring them with you into the city, then recheck them for your onward flight.

**Check whether the stopover affects your fare's flexibility or change policy.** Adding a stopover, even a free one, sometimes shifts your ticket into a different fare category with different rules around changes or cancellations — worth confirming before committing.

**Consider the season and weather in the stopover city specifically**, since you're choosing this destination based on airline routing rather than starting from "where do I want to go," it's worth checking that your travel dates actually suit that particular city.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a different type of ticket to add a stopover?**
Not usually — most stopover programmes are built into the standard booking flow for eligible routes, rather than requiring a separate or special fare type. The stopover option typically just appears as a step during booking on eligible routes.

**Q: Can I add a stopover to a one-way flight?**
This depends on the specific airline's programme — some support stopovers on one-way tickets, others require a return or multi-city booking structure. Check the specific programme's terms.

**Q: Does a stopover count as a separate visa entry?**
Generally yes, in the sense that you're formally entering that country for the duration of your stopover, so its standard entry and visa requirements typically apply, regardless of the flight being booked as one continuous journey.

**Q: What happens if I want to extend my stopover beyond the programme's maximum?**
This usually isn't possible within the free programme itself — if you want a longer stay than the maximum offered, you'd typically need to book the stopover city as your own separate multi-city leg rather than through the formal stopover programme.

## Final Thoughts

A free stopover programme is one of the more genuinely valuable tricks available to travellers whose route happens to pass through an airline's hub city — effectively adding an extra destination to a trip you were taking anyway, at no additional flight cost. The catch is that it only works if your ideal extra city happens to be one of the limited hubs offering the programme; for anywhere else, building your own multi-city itinerary remains the more flexible, if not quite as free, option.
