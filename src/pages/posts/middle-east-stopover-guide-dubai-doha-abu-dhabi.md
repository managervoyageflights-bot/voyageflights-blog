# Middle East Stopover Guide: Dubai, Doha, Abu Dhabi

Dubai, Doha, and Abu Dhabi are each major long-haul aviation hubs in their own right — Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Etihad respectively all operate extensive route networks through these three cities, which means many travellers already pass through at least one of them en route somewhere else. Combining all three into a single multi-city trip takes advantage of this: short regional flights, dramatically different experiences in each city, and some of the best-connected airports in the world bookending the whole journey.

This guide covers how to combine the three, how long to spend in each, and what makes them each worth visiting in their own right.

---

## Why This Combination Works

The three cities sit within a short flying distance of each other — Dubai to Doha is around an hour, Abu Dhabi to Dubai barely 45 minutes by air (and under 90 minutes by road, making a direct drive a genuine alternative to flying for this specific leg). This makes the region unusually compact for a multi-city trip spanning three distinct countries.

Despite their geographic closeness and some surface similarities (all three are modern Gulf cities built substantially within the last few decades), each has a genuinely different character: Dubai is the most maximalist and tourist-oriented, Abu Dhabi more measured and culturally significant as the UAE's capital, and Doha increasingly positioning itself around art, culture, and a more curated luxury experience.

---

## Suggested Route and Timing

**London → Dubai → Abu Dhabi → Doha → London**

This order works well geographically, moving from Dubai westward along the coast to Abu Dhabi before the short hop to Doha, avoiding backtracking.

**Total trip length: 8–12 days**

- **Dubai:** 3–4 days
- **Abu Dhabi:** 2–3 days
- **Doha:** 3–4 days

This is also one of the more flexible combinations for a shorter trip — even 6–7 days total gives a reasonable taste of all three cities, since the connections between them add minimal travel time compared to longer-haul multi-city routes.

---

## Dubai: 3–4 Days

### Getting There
London → Dubai (DXB) is approximately 7 hours direct, served extensively by Emirates alongside British Airways and others.

### What to Prioritise

**Downtown Dubai** — the Burj Khalifa (book the "At the Top" observation deck in advance for sunset), Dubai Mall, and the Dubai Fountain show in the evening.

**Old Dubai (Deira and Bur Dubai)** — the Dubai Creek, traditional abra boat crossings, and the gold and spice souks, offering a genuinely different atmosphere from the ultramodern downtown.

**Dubai Marina and JBR Beach** — for a more relaxed day combining beach time with Dubai's striking waterfront high-rise skyline.

**A desert safari** — a half-day or evening excursion into the surrounding desert, typically including dune driving, a traditional camp experience, and dinner, is one of the most popular excursions and gives a sense of the landscape beyond the city itself.

### Practical Notes
- Dubai is extremely hot from May to September (regularly above 40°C); October to April is considerably more comfortable for outdoor sightseeing.
- The Dubai Metro is efficient and air-conditioned, making it a comfortable way to get around during the hotter months.
- Dress codes are more relaxed than some neighbouring countries but still worth respecting, particularly away from beach and hotel areas.

## Abu Dhabi: 2–3 Days

### Getting There
Dubai → Abu Dhabi is a roughly 45-minute flight, though given the short distance (around 130km), many travellers opt for a taxi or bus instead — the road journey takes about 90 minutes to 2 hours depending on traffic, and avoids the time overhead of airport check-in and security for such a short hop.

### What to Prioritise

**Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque** — one of the most architecturally significant mosques in the world and genuinely one of the standout sights in the entire region, open to non-Muslim visitors with appropriate dress (robes are provided for those who need them).

**Louvre Abu Dhabi** — a striking piece of architecture housing an internationally curated collection, part of Abu Dhabi's deliberate positioning as a cultural capital for the region.

**Qasr Al Watan (Presidential Palace)** — opened to the public, offering insight into Emirati governance and culture alongside genuinely impressive architecture and interior design.

**Yas Island** — home to Ferrari World, Yas Waterworld, and Yas Marina Circuit (Abu Dhabi's Formula 1 venue), worth a visit if travelling with family or interested in a more activity-focused day.

### Practical Notes
- Abu Dhabi feels noticeably calmer and less overtly commercial than Dubai, with a stronger emphasis on cultural and governmental significance as the UAE's capital.
- The same UAE visa that covers Dubai applies here, since both are part of the same country — no separate entry requirements between the two.
- Abu Dhabi's attractions are more spread out than Dubai's, making a rental car or taxi more practical than relying solely on public transport for a short visit.

## Doha: 3–4 Days

### Getting There
Abu Dhabi → Doha is approximately 1h15 by air; alternatively, routing back through Dubai first (roughly 1 hour to Doha from there) is also common depending on flight availability and pricing.

### What to Prioritise

**The Museum of Islamic Art** — widely regarded as one of the finest museums in the Middle East, both for its architecture (designed by I. M. Pei) and its collection, set on its own promontory with views back across Doha's skyline.

**Souq Waqif** — Doha's restored traditional market, considerably more atmospheric in the evening when temperatures drop and the souq comes alive with restaurants, shisha cafes, and falconry shops.

**The Corniche** — Doha's waterfront promenade, offering a classic view of the West Bay skyline and a pleasant walking or cycling route, particularly in the cooler evening hours.

**National Museum of Qatar** — a striking, desert-rose-inspired piece of architecture housing exhibits on Qatari history and culture, a relatively recent and significant addition to the city's cultural offerings.

### Practical Notes
- Qatar has invested heavily in cultural infrastructure in recent years, particularly following the 2022 World Cup, and Doha's museum and cultural scene is genuinely a highlight of the broader Gulf region.
- Qatar's climate follows a similar pattern to Dubai and Abu Dhabi — October to April is considerably more comfortable than the intense summer heat.
- Public transport (the Doha Metro) is modern and efficient, covering most areas relevant to visitors.

## Sample 10-Day Itinerary

**Days 1–4: Dubai**
- Day 1: Arrive, evening in Old Dubai
- Day 2: Downtown Dubai, Burj Khalifa at sunset
- Day 3: Desert safari
- Day 4: Dubai Marina, free morning, afternoon transfer to Abu Dhabi (road or flight)

**Days 4–6: Abu Dhabi**
- Day 4: Arrive, evening at leisure
- Day 5: Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, Louvre Abu Dhabi
- Day 6: Qasr Al Watan or Yas Island, afternoon flight to Doha

**Days 6–10: Doha**
- Day 6: Arrive, evening at Souq Waqif
- Day 7: Museum of Islamic Art, Corniche walk
- Day 8: National Museum of Qatar
- Day 9: Free day, final shopping or cultural visits
- Day 10: Depart

## Visa and Entry Notes

UAE and Qatar have separate visa policies, since they're different countries, despite the short distance and cultural similarities between them. UK passport holders currently receive visa-free entry to both for standard tourist stays, though specific durations and conditions can change — always verify current requirements on the relevant official government sources before travelling, since Gulf state entry policies have been updated at various points in recent years.

---

## Budget Considerations

All three cities sit at a broadly similar price point — comfortably mid-to-high range by international standards, with considerable flexibility depending on accommodation choices. Dubai generally offers the widest range of options from budget to ultra-luxury; Abu Dhabi and Doha both skew toward the higher end, with fewer budget-oriented options overall, though this is shifting somewhat as Doha in particular develops more tourism infrastructure.

---

## Final Thoughts

The combination of Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Doha works particularly well as a multi-city trip precisely because of how close together they are geographically, despite being genuinely distinct destinations with different characters — Dubai's scale and spectacle, Abu Dhabi's cultural weight as a capital, and Doha's increasingly confident positioning around art and architecture. The short hops between them mean minimal time lost to travel, leaving more of a relatively compact trip for actually experiencing each city.
