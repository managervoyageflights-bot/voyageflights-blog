# The Best Multi-City Flight Routes in Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is one of the world's most rewarding regions for multi-city travel. Distances between countries are short, budget airlines connect dozens of city pairs at remarkably low fares, and the variety of experience from one destination to the next is extraordinary — ancient temples, colonial cities, volcanic islands, jungle, and some of the best food on the planet.

The challenge isn't finding flights. It's knowing which combinations work well together, which order makes geographic sense, and how to pace a trip so you're not spending every other day in an airport.

This guide covers the best multi-city routes in Southeast Asia, with suggested timings, practical airport tips, and advice on what to expect at each stop.

---

## Why Southeast Asia Is Perfect for Multi-City Travel

A few things make the region uniquely well-suited to multi-city itineraries:

**Short flight distances.** Bangkok to Singapore is 2h30. Kuala Lumpur to Bali is 2h45. Ho Chi Minh City to Hanoi is 2h. These are short enough that flying between countries feels no more disruptive than a domestic hop in Europe.

**Exceptional budget airline networks.** AirAsia, VietJet, Lion Air, Citilink, and others offer one-way fares between regional cities that are often under £30 — sometimes under £10 if booked early. The economics of piecing together a multi-city itinerary from individual legs are extremely favourable.

**Enormous cultural variety.** Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, and the Philippines each offer a completely different travel experience. A two-week trip that combines two or three of these countries is far more rewarding than spending the same time in one place.

## The Classic Indochina Loop

**Bangkok → Chiang Mai → Hanoi → Ho Chi Minh City → Bangkok**

The most popular multi-city route in Southeast Asia, and for good reason. This loop takes you through Thailand's two great cities and the length of Vietnam, finishing back in Bangkok for the flight home.

**Suggested timing (2–3 weeks):**
- Bangkok: Days 1–4
- Bangkok → Chiang Mai: Day 5 (1h20 flight on Thai Airways or AirAsia)
- Chiang Mai: Days 5–8
- Chiang Mai → Hanoi: Day 9 (2h30 flight, sometimes via Bangkok)
- Hanoi: Days 9–12
- Hanoi → Ho Chi Minh City: Day 13 (2h direct flight on Vietnam Airlines or VietJet)
- Ho Chi Minh City: Days 13–17
- Ho Chi Minh City → Bangkok: Day 18 (1h50 flight — return to Bangkok for long-haul home)

**Why it works:** The route moves geographically from north to south through Vietnam, avoiding any backtracking. Bangkok serves as both the entry and exit point, making long-haul connections to London straightforward.

**Tips:**
- The overnight train from Hanoi to Hội An (via Đà Nẵng) is one of Southeast Asia's great rail journeys and a good alternative to flying between the two Vietnamese cities if you have time.
- Chiang Mai's Sunday Walking Street and Doi Suthep temple are highlights; book accommodation early in high season (November–February).
- Ho Chi Minh City's War Remnants Museum and the Cu Chi Tunnels are essential but emotionally demanding — give yourself time.

## The Island-Hopping Route

**Kuala Lumpur → Bali → Lombok → Singapore → Kuala Lumpur**

![Terraced rice fields in Bali with palm trees and traditional temple](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1537996194471-e657df975ab4?w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)
*Photo by Artem Bali on Unsplash*

For those who want more beach and less city, this route combines Malaysia's cosmopolitan capital with two of Indonesia's finest islands before finishing in Singapore.

**Suggested timing (2 weeks):**
- Kuala Lumpur: Days 1–3
- Kuala Lumpur → Bali (Denpasar): Day 4 (2h45 on AirAsia or Batik Air)
- Bali: Days 4–9
- Bali → Lombok: Day 10 (35-minute flight or 4h fast boat — boat is more scenic)
- Lombok: Days 10–13
- Lombok → Singapore: Day 14 (3h flight via Kuala Lumpur or direct)
- Singapore: Days 14–16
- Singapore → London: Day 17 (13h direct from Changi on Singapore Airlines or BA)

**Why it works:** Kuala Lumpur is a natural hub for AirAsia connections across the region. Bali and Lombok offer contrasting island experiences — Bali is lively and well-developed, Lombok is quieter and less touristy. Singapore closes the loop as one of the world's best-connected airports for the return home.

**Tips:**
- Singapore's Changi Airport (SIN) is consistently rated the world's best airport and has some of the most reliable long-haul connections back to London.
- Lombok's Gili Islands (Gili Trawangan, Gili Air, Gili Meno) are accessible by boat from the main island and are among the best snorkelling spots in Indonesia.
- Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KUL) and the budget terminal KLIA2 are separate buildings — check which terminal your flight uses.

## The Angkor and Mekong Route

**Bangkok → Siem Reap → Phnom Penh → Ho Chi Minh City → Singapore**

A route that follows the arc of the Mekong River through Cambodia and into southern Vietnam, taking in one of the world's great archaeological sites along the way.

**Suggested timing (2 weeks):**
- Bangkok: Days 1–2
- Bangkok → Siem Reap: Day 3 (1h direct flight on Bangkok Airways or AirAsia)
- Siem Reap: Days 3–6 (Angkor Wat and surrounding temples)
- Siem Reap → Phnom Penh: Day 7 (45-minute flight or 6h scenic boat along the Tonlé Sap River)
- Phnom Penh: Days 7–9
- Phnom Penh → Ho Chi Minh City: Day 10 (1h flight or 6h bus via the Mekong border crossing)
- Ho Chi Minh City: Days 10–13
- Ho Chi Minh City → Singapore: Day 14 (2h flight — connect home from Changi)

**Why it works:** The route follows a natural geographic arc from Thailand through Cambodia into Vietnam, moving steadily eastward with no backtracking. Each destination adds a distinct layer to the experience — ancient civilisation, colonial history, and modern urban energy.

**Tips:**
- Angkor Wat requires at least two full days to explore properly. The temple complex is vast and the individual sites — Bayon, Ta Prohm, Preah Khan — each deserve time.
- A temple pass for Angkor covers multiple days and is purchased at the main gate. Pre-dawn visits to watch the sunrise over Angkor Wat are popular but crowded — arriving 30 minutes before sunrise gives you the best view with fewer people.
- Cambodia requires a visa (e-visa available online before travel for UK passport holders).
- The Phnom Penh → Ho Chi Minh City bus crossing is a classic Southeast Asia overland experience if you have the time and patience for border formalities.

## The Philippines and Borneo Extension

**Singapore → Manila → Palawan → Kota Kinabalu → Singapore**

A more adventurous route combining the Philippines' most beautiful island with the wildlife-rich rainforests of Malaysian Borneo.

**Suggested timing (2 weeks):**
- Singapore: Days 1–2
- Singapore → Manila: Day 3 (3h30 on Philippine Airlines, Cebu Pacific, or Singapore Airlines)
- Manila: Day 3 (transit — Manila is worth a night but not the focus)
- Manila → Puerto Princesa, Palawan: Day 4 (1h30 domestic flight on Cebu Pacific)
- Palawan (El Nido / Coron): Days 4–9
- Palawan → Kota Kinabalu: Day 10 (via Manila or direct on AirAsia)
- Kota Kinabalu: Days 10–13 (Mount Kinabalu, Sepilok orangutan sanctuary, river safaris)
- Kota Kinabalu → Singapore: Day 14 (2h30 on AirAsia or Malaysia Airlines — connect home from Changi)

**Why it works:** Palawan consistently ranks among the world's most beautiful island destinations — the limestone karsts and turquoise lagoons of El Nido and the wreck diving of Coron are genuinely extraordinary. Kota Kinabalu in Sabah offers a completely different experience: jungle, wildlife, and one of Southeast Asia's most challenging treks (Mount Kinabalu).

**Tips:**
- El Nido and Coron in Palawan are reached by small aircraft or boat from Puerto Princesa. The island-hopping tours in El Nido (Tours A, B, C, and D) are essential.
- The Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre near Sandakan is one of the best places in the world to see wild orangutans at close range. Combine with a night river cruise to see proboscis monkeys and pygmy elephants.
- Philippine visa: UK passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 30 days. Malaysia is also visa-free.

## The Myanmar and Laos Circuit

**Bangkok → Yangon → Bagan → Luang Prabang → Vientiane → Bangkok**

![Ancient Buddhist temples at sunrise in Bagan, Myanmar](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1558618047-f4e60e2b8acd?w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)
*Photo by Mathew Schwartz on Unsplash*

A slower, more contemplative route through two of Southeast Asia's least-visited and most atmospheric countries. This is a trip for travellers who want to step away from the tourist trail.

**Suggested timing (3 weeks):**
- Bangkok: Days 1–2
- Bangkok → Yangon: Day 3 (1h45 on Bangkok Airways or Myanmar Airways International)
- Yangon: Days 3–5
- Yangon → Bagan: Day 6 (1h domestic flight on Myanmar National Airlines)
- Bagan: Days 6–9 (temple plains at sunrise — one of the great sights in Asia)
- Bagan → Luang Prabang: Day 10 (via Yangon or Mandalay — allow a full travel day)
- Luang Prabang: Days 10–14
- Luang Prabang → Vientiane: Day 15 (1h flight or the Mekong slow boat — 2 days)
- Vientiane: Days 15–17
- Vientiane → Bangkok: Day 18 (1h30 flight on Lao Airlines or Bangkok Airways)

**Why it works:** Myanmar and Laos are two of the most rewarding countries in Southeast Asia precisely because they remain less visited than their neighbours. Bagan's temple plains rival Angkor Wat in scale and atmosphere; Luang Prabang is arguably the most beautiful small city in the region.

**Important note on Myanmar:** The political situation in Myanmar has been unstable since the 2021 military coup. Check the UK Foreign Office travel advice (gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/myanmar) before planning any travel there, and consider whether it is appropriate given the current context.

**Tips:**
- Luang Prabang's alms-giving ceremony (tak bat) takes place every morning at dawn. Watch respectfully from a distance — tourist participation is generally discouraged.
- Laos requires a visa on arrival for UK passport holders (30-day single entry).
- The Mekong slow boat from Huay Xai to Luang Prabang (2 days) is a classic Southeast Asia experience and a good alternative to flying if you have the time.

## Practical Tips for Multi-City Travel in Southeast Asia

**Book budget airline legs separately.** AirAsia, VietJet, and Cebu Pacific often have the best regional fares but don't always appear on multi-city booking platforms. Search each leg individually on these carriers' own websites and compare against bundled fares.

**Use Singapore or Kuala Lumpur as your hub.** Both Changi (SIN) and KLIA (KUL) are outstanding hub airports with dense regional networks and excellent long-haul connections back to London. Building your itinerary to depart or return via one of these two cities simplifies connections considerably.

**Allow more time than you think you need.** Southeast Asia rewards slow travel. The best experiences — a sunrise over Angkor Wat, a cooking class in Chiang Mai, a day on a longtail boat in Palawan — happen when you're not rushing to the next destination.

**Check visa requirements carefully.** Requirements vary significantly by nationality and destination, and they change. Always verify current requirements on the official embassy website or the UK Foreign Office travel advice pages before booking.

**Plan around the seasons.** Southeast Asia's weather is complex and varies by country and region. As a general guide:
- **November to February:** Best for most of Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, and Laos
- **March to May:** Hot and dry across much of the region — manageable but demanding
- **June to October:** Monsoon season in much of the region, though Bali and eastern Indonesia remain relatively dry

**Use VoyageFlights to check connection times.** Regional airports in Southeast Asia vary enormously in size and efficiency. Siem Reap, Luang Prabang, and Lombok are small and manageable; Manila, Bangkok Suvarnabhumi, and Kuala Lumpur are large hubs where you need more time for connections. Always verify that your layovers are realistic before booking.
