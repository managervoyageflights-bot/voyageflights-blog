# Southeast Asia Multi-City Itinerary: Bangkok, Bali, Singapore

Bangkok, Bali, and Singapore form one of the most popular and best-balanced multi-city combinations in Southeast Asia — and for good reason. Each offers a genuinely different experience: Bangkok's chaotic energy and street food, Bali's beaches and rice terraces, Singapore's polish and efficiency. Together they cover city, island, and metropolis in a single trip, with short, affordable flights connecting all three.

This guide gives you a complete, practical itinerary: how long to spend in each place, how to move between them, what to prioritise, and the details that make the difference between a smooth trip and a rushed one.

---

## Why This Combination Works

Geographically, the three cities form a loose triangle across mainland and island Southeast Asia, all within 2–4 hours of flying time from each other. None of the connections require backtracking, and budget carriers (AirAsia, Scoot, Jetstar) operate frequent, affordable services on every leg.

More importantly, the contrast between destinations is what makes the trip memorable. Bangkok is intense, fast-paced, and overwhelming in the best way. Bali slows everything down — beaches, temples, rice paddies, and a famously laid-back pace of life. Singapore closes the loop with order, efficiency, and some of the best food in Asia in a totally different register from Bangkok's street stalls.

---

## Suggested Route and Timing

**London → Bangkok → Bali → Singapore → London**

This order moves geographically from north to south, finishing in Singapore — one of the world's best-connected airports for the long-haul flight home.

**Total trip length: 16–18 days** (can be compressed to 12 at a push, or extended to 3 weeks comfortably)

- **Bangkok:** 5 days
- **Bali:** 8 days
- **Singapore:** 3–4 days

---

## Bangkok: 5 Days

### Getting There
London → Bangkok (Suvarnabhumi, BKK) is roughly 11–12 hours direct, served by Thai Airways, British Airways, and Qatar Airways/Emirates via a Gulf hub if you prefer to break up the journey.

### What to Prioritise

**The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew** — Bangkok's most famous sight, best visited early morning before the heat and crowds build. Dress code is strictly enforced (shoulders and knees covered).

**Wat Arun and the Chao Phraya River** — best seen from a river ferry, which is also one of the cheapest and most enjoyable ways to get around the old city.

**Chatuchak Weekend Market** — one of the world's largest markets, open Saturday and Sunday. Plan a half-day minimum; it's easy to get lost (in a good way).

**Street food in Chinatown (Yaowarat)** — Bangkok's street food culture is legendary, and Chinatown after dark is one of the best places to experience it.

**A day trip to Ayutthaya** — the ruined former capital, about 90 minutes north of Bangkok by train or organised tour, and a worthwhile contrast to the modern city.

### Practical Notes
- Bangkok's BTS Skytrain and MRT subway are efficient and air-conditioned — far more pleasant than navigating traffic by road during the day.
- Tuk-tuks are fun but agree the fare before getting in; metered taxis are usually cheaper.
- April is the hottest month and best avoided if possible; November to February is the most comfortable period.

## Bali: 8 Days

### Getting There
Bangkok → Denpasar (Bali, DPS) is approximately 3h45 direct on AirAsia, Thai AirAsia, or Garuda Indonesia.

### Suggested Sub-Itinerary Within Bali

Bali rewards splitting your time across at least two or three areas rather than basing yourself in one place for the whole stay.

**Seminyak or Canggu (2–3 days)** — Bali's beach and surf culture, good restaurants, sunset bars along the coast. Canggu in particular has a strong digital nomad and surf scene with excellent cafes.

**Ubud (3 days)** — the cultural heart of Bali: rice terraces (Tegalalang is the most famous, though increasingly crowded — Jatiluwih is a quieter alternative), the Sacred Monkey Forest, traditional dance performances, and a noticeably cooler climate due to the elevation.

**Uluwatu or the Bukit Peninsula (2–3 days)** — dramatic clifftop temples (Uluwatu Temple, especially at sunset), world-class surf breaks, and a more rugged, less developed coastline than Seminyak.

### Practical Notes
- Renting a scooter is the most common way to get around Bali, but traffic and road conditions can be challenging for inexperienced riders. Hiring a private driver for day trips is a safer, still affordable alternative.
- Bali's wet season (November–March) brings heavy but generally short downpours; the dry season (April–October) is more reliable for beach time but also more crowded, particularly July–August.
- The Gili Islands (off Lombok, reachable by fast boat from Bali) are a worthwhile 2–3 day extension if you have additional time and want a slower, car-free island experience.

## Singapore: 3–4 Days

### Getting There
Denpasar → Singapore (Changi, SIN) is approximately 2h45, served by Scoot, Singapore Airlines, and Jetstar Asia.

### What to Prioritise

**Gardens by the Bay** — the Supertree Grove and Cloud Forest/Flower Dome conservatories are Singapore's signature modern attraction. Visit the Supertrees at night for the light show.

**Hawker centres** — Singapore's hawker food culture (several centres hold Michelin recognition) offers some of the best-value, highest-quality food in Asia. Maxwell Food Centre and Lau Pa Sat are excellent starting points.

**Marina Bay Sands SkyPark and the Merlion waterfront** — for the classic skyline view, particularly impressive at dusk.

**Chinatown, Little India, and Kampong Glam** — each offers a distinct cultural neighbourhood within a short MRT ride of each other, and together give a sense of Singapore's multicultural character beyond the futuristic skyline.

**A half-day at Sentosa Island** — beaches, Universal Studios Singapore if travelling with family, and the cable car connecting it to the mainland.

### Practical Notes
- Singapore is significantly more expensive than Bangkok or Bali — budget accordingly, particularly for accommodation.
- The MRT system is excellent, clean, and easy to navigate — a contactless card or phone payment works directly on the gates.
- Strict laws around littering, jaywalking, and chewing gum import are genuinely enforced; Singapore's reputation for order is well-earned.
- Changi Airport's Jewel complex (with its indoor waterfall) is worth visiting even if you're not flying through it specifically — easily reached from the city centre.

## Sample Day-by-Day Itinerary

**Days 1–5: Bangkok**
- Day 1: Arrive, settle in, evening at Yaowarat (Chinatown)
- Day 2: Grand Palace, Wat Pho, river ferry to Wat Arun
- Day 3: Ayutthaya day trip
- Day 4: Chatuchak Market (if a weekend), Jim Thompson House
- Day 5: Free morning, afternoon flight to Bali

**Days 6–8: Seminyak/Canggu**
- Beach time, surf lessons if interested, sunset at a beach club

**Days 9–11: Ubud**
- Rice terraces, Monkey Forest, traditional dance, day trip to a waterfall (Tegenungan or Tibumana)

**Days 12–13: Uluwatu**
- Uluwatu Temple at sunset, beach days at Padang Padang or Bingin

**Day 14: Free day in Bali / travel buffer, evening flight to Singapore**

**Days 15–17: Singapore**
- Day 15: Gardens by the Bay, Marina Bay waterfront
- Day 16: Chinatown, hawker centre lunch, Little India in the evening
- Day 17: Sentosa half-day, free time, departure preparations

**Day 18: Fly home**

## Visa and Entry Notes

UK passport holders can currently enter Thailand, Indonesia, and Singapore visa-free for standard tourist stays, though specific durations and conditions can change — always verify current requirements on the relevant embassy website before booking, particularly for Indonesia, where rules around visa-on-arrival fees and durations have shifted in recent years.

---

## Budget Considerations

Costs vary significantly between the three destinations:

**Bangkok:** Generally the most affordable of the three — street food meals can cost £2–4, mid-range hotels £25–50/night.

**Bali:** Mid-range overall, though highly variable by area — Canggu and Seminyak are pricier than Ubud or more rural areas. Villas with private pools are often surprisingly affordable by Western standards (£30–80/night for a good standard).

**Singapore:** The most expensive leg by a clear margin — expect hotel prices comparable to major European cities, though hawker centre food remains excellent value even on a tight budget.

---

## Final Thoughts

Bangkok, Bali, and Singapore work exceptionally well together precisely because of how different they are — frenetic street life, island calm, and polished modernity in a single trip. The connections between them are short, affordable, and frequent, making this one of the most accessible multi-city combinations in Southeast Asia for a first-time visitor to the region.

Sixteen to eighteen days gives each destination proper time without rushing; if your schedule is tighter, Bangkok and Singapore can be trimmed slightly more easily than Bali, where the appeal is largely about slowing down.
