# Heathrow Airport Layover Guide: Everything You Need to Know

Heathrow (LHR) is one of the world's busiest hubs for long-haul connections, and its five-terminal layout is genuinely one of the more demanding airport structures to navigate on a tight connection — considerably more so than a single-terminal or even a multi-concourse-within-one-terminal setup like Dubai's. Understanding which terminal your flights actually use, and what a transfer between terminals really involves, makes a significant difference to how comfortable your Heathrow layover is.

This guide covers the terminal layout, realistic connection times, what inter-terminal transfers actually involve, and how to make the most of longer layovers.

---

## Heathrow's Terminal Layout

Heathrow operates five terminals, and unlike some hubs where one airline dominates a single terminal almost entirely, Heathrow's terminal allocation is split across different airline alliances and individual carriers.

**Terminal 2** ("The Queen's Terminal") is home to Star Alliance carriers, including United, Lufthansa, Air Canada, and several others, alongside a handful of other airlines.

**Terminal 3** hosts a mix of carriers including Virgin Atlantic, Delta, Emirates, and various others not aligned with Terminal 5's Oneworld grouping.

**Terminal 4** serves SkyTeam carriers including Air France, KLM, and several others, plus a number of additional airlines.

**Terminal 5** is British Airways' dedicated home terminal and also serves Iberia and other Oneworld carriers.

**Terminal 1 no longer operates** as a passenger terminal (having closed some years ago), though it's occasionally referenced in older material.

**Check both your inbound and outbound flight's specific terminal before travelling.** Because Heathrow's terminals are aligned more by airline and alliance than by any other logic, a connection within the same alliance is far more likely to stay within one terminal than a connection between unrelated carriers.

## Realistic Layover Times at Heathrow

**Same terminal (e.g., British Airways to British Airways, both via Terminal 5):** 75 minutes is the airport's practical minimum, but 90 minutes is a more comfortable target given Terminal 5's own considerable size — it's split across a main building and satellite piers, and gate-to-gate walking distances within the "same" terminal can still be significant.

**Terminal change required:** Budget 2 hours minimum, and closer to 2.5 hours if your connection also involves clearing security again or a longer walk to the inter-terminal transfer point. Terminal transfers at Heathrow typically involve either a dedicated bus service or, in some cases, the Heathrow Express/Elizabeth line connection between terminal areas — neither is instantaneous, and the process adds meaningfully more time than travellers moving between terminals for the first time often expect.

**International to international with immigration:** Allow 2–2.5 hours, since UK immigration queue times for connecting passengers can vary considerably depending on time of day and how many long-haul flights have landed around the same time.

**Connections involving Terminal 5's satellite piers specifically:** Terminal 5 itself is split into the main terminal building and two satellite piers (5B and 5C), connected by an internal transit train. The train itself runs frequently — typically every few minutes — so it's not usually a source of delay on its own, but the walk to the platform, the ride, and the walk to your gate at the other end can still add a meaningful chunk of time to what looks like a same-terminal connection on paper. Worth building in extra time rather than assuming it's a simple walk, even though the train service itself is generally quick and reliable.

## What an Inter-Terminal Transfer at Heathrow Actually Involves

This is where Heathrow differs most from many other major hubs, and it's worth understanding in advance if your itinerary involves two different terminals.

**If you're transiting airside** (not needing to clear UK immigration, both flights are connections), you'll typically use a dedicated airside transfer bus between terminals, which can take 15–30 minutes depending on which specific terminals are involved, plus walking and waiting time at each end.

**If you need to clear immigration** (arriving from outside the common travel area and connecting to a different terminal), you'll go through UK immigration, potentially collect and recheck baggage, and then make your way to the new terminal — a meaningfully longer process than an airside-only transfer.

**Security re-screening is often required** when changing terminals at Heathrow, even for airside transfers, since each terminal operates its own security zone. Factor this into your buffer time as its own distinct step, not just walking or bus time.

## What to Do With a Short Layover (Under 3 Hours)

With a short connection, especially one involving a terminal change, staying focused on making your connection is the priority rather than planning activities.

**If you're same-terminal**, Heathrow's terminals each have a reasonable range of shops, restaurants, and seating, so a short wait is manageable without needing to venture anywhere specific.

**Terminal 5 in particular** has a notably wide range of retail and dining given its size and British Airways' dominant presence there.

**If a terminal change is involved**, treat any remaining time after the transfer as a bonus rather than something to plan around — Heathrow's inter-terminal logistics are reliable but not instant, and it's easy to underestimate how much of a short layover they consume.

## What to Do With a Medium Layover (3–6 Hours)

This range gives some breathing room to use Heathrow's amenities properly, even accounting for a possible terminal change.

**Airport lounges** are available via day pass across all terminals, even without airline status, and offer a considerably more comfortable environment than the main terminal seating areas, particularly useful for recovering from or preparing for a long-haul flight.

**Heathrow's terminals generally have good wifi and a wide range of food options**, making a few hours here relatively comfortable compared to some other major hubs.

**Leaving the airport becomes marginally possible toward the upper end of this range** (5–6 hours) if your connection is entirely within one terminal, though it's tight once London's transport times and UK immigration are factored in — see the long layover section below for a more comfortable version of this.

## What to Do With a Long Layover (6+ Hours)

With 6 or more hours, and particularly closer to 8+, leaving the airport to see something of London becomes a genuinely worthwhile option — Heathrow's excellent public transport links into central London make this more feasible than at many other major hub airports.

**Realistic time in central London:** The Elizabeth line and Heathrow Express both connect Heathrow directly to central London in around 15–45 minutes depending on your specific stop, considerably faster than many other major airports' city links. After accounting for immigration, the journey each way, and a sensible buffer before your return flight, expect roughly 3–5 usable hours in London from a 7–8 hour layover.

**What's realistically reachable:** Central London landmarks within a reasonable radius of a major Underground or Elizabeth line station — for example, a walk around Westminster, the South Bank, or Covent Garden — are all feasible within a well-planned long layover, without needing to venture particularly far from central transport links.

**A few practical considerations specific to London:** UK immigration for eligible nationalities is generally efficient, but queue times vary; London's public transport is excellent but can be affected by weekend engineering works or occasional disruption, so building in extra buffer for the return journey is sensible; and London's famously changeable weather is worth checking before you commit to an outdoor-heavy plan.

**Left luggage facilities** are available at Heathrow if you'd rather not carry bags into the city for a few hours, which can make a short London visit considerably more comfortable.

## Practical Tips Specific to Heathrow

**Always double-check your terminal shortly before travelling, not just when you book.** Airlines occasionally shift terminal allocations, and Heathrow's split-by-alliance structure means this matters more here than at airports where one airline occupies an entire terminal consistently.

**If your connection involves British Airways on both legs, you'll very likely stay within Terminal 5**, which is generally the most straightforward same-terminal scenario at Heathrow given its scale and self-contained facilities.

**Weekday morning and early evening periods tend to see the highest passenger volumes** at Heathrow, which can mean longer queues at security and immigration — worth factoring in if your layover falls during these windows.

**London's weather and general unpredictability is worth building buffer around** if you're leaving the airport — public transport delays, while not the norm, do happen, and Heathrow's own inter-terminal systems, while reliable, add a layer of complexity to getting back with margin to spare.

**If you're eligible to use the e-gates at UK immigration, it's well worth doing so.** A growing number of nationalities can use the automated e-gates rather than a staffed immigration desk, which is typically considerably faster, especially when several long-haul flights land around the same time and staffed desk queues build up. This is particularly useful on a tight connection involving a terminal change, where every few minutes saved at immigration meaningfully improves your buffer. Eligibility depends on your passport and is worth checking before you travel, since it can change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is 75 minutes enough for a Heathrow layover?**
Only for a straightforward same-terminal connection with no immigration requirement — and even then, 90 minutes is a more comfortable target given how large individual Heathrow terminals are. For any terminal change, 75 minutes is not enough.

**Q: Which terminal does British Airways use at Heathrow?**
Terminal 5, almost exclusively, along with other Oneworld alliance carriers such as Iberia.

**Q: How do I get between Heathrow terminals?**
Via a dedicated transfer bus for most airside connections, or via the Elizabeth line/Heathrow Express connection in some cases — neither is instantaneous, so budget real time for this rather than treating it as a quick walk.

**Q: Is it worth leaving Heathrow for a long layover to see London?**
Often yes, given Heathrow's fast rail links into central London — considerably more feasible than the equivalent excursion at many other major hub airports, provided you have at least 6-7 hours and your immigration situation is straightforward.

## Final Thoughts

Heathrow is a highly capable airport for connecting passengers, but its five-terminal, alliance-based structure means the terminal your specific flights use matters more here than at many other major hubs. Check both your inbound and outbound terminal in advance, budget real time for any inter-terminal transfer rather than treating it as a quick walk, and take advantage of Heathrow's excellent London transport links if you have a genuinely long layover to work with.
