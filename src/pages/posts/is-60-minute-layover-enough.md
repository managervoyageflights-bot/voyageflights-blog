# Is a 60-Minute Layover Enough? A Minute-by-Minute Breakdown

Sixty minutes sits right at the edge of what most airports and booking systems will allow as a minimum connection time — and right at the edge of what's realistically comfortable. Rather than a general rule, the honest answer depends on exactly how those 60 minutes get used, which is easiest to see by breaking the connection down step by step.

This guide walks through where that hour actually goes, in a fairly typical scenario, and what specifically needs to go right for it to work.

---

## The Short Answer

**A 60-minute layover carries a real risk of missing your connection, even in the best-case scenario.** As the breakdown below shows, a genuinely comfortable connection uses most or all of that hour even when everything goes exactly to plan — deplaning on time, walking without delay, no unexpected queues. There's very little margin left over for anything to go even slightly wrong, and long-haul flights in particular run late often enough that this isn't a remote possibility.

It can work — same terminal, no immigration check, same airline or close alliance partners, an on-time inbound flight — but "can work" is different from "reliably will work." If you have any choice in the matter, treat 60 minutes as the connection you take when no better option exists, not one you'd actively choose.

It's also worth being clear that even when a tight 60-minute connection does technically work, "making it" isn't the same as making it comfortably. Cutting it close often means boarding among the last passengers, arriving at the gate as final boarding is called or after it's already begun — which in turn means overhead bin space is likely already full, and you may need to gate-check a bag you'd planned to carry on, adding time and inconvenience at both ends of that flight. A connection that "works" in the sense of not being missed can still come with these smaller costs, which are worth factoring in alongside the more serious risk of missing the flight entirely.

## Where the 60 Minutes Actually Goes

Here's a realistic breakdown of a same-terminal, no-immigration connection:

**Minutes 0–8: Deplaning.** This varies more than people expect — a full long-haul aircraft, a seat toward the back, or a slow jet bridge connection can easily push this past 10 minutes. A seat near the front on a smaller aircraft might have you off in 3–4 minutes.

**Minutes 8–20: Walking to your new gate.** This is the biggest variable of all. At a compact airport with adjacent gates, this might take 5 minutes. At a large terminal with your new gate at the opposite end, or requiring an internal train (common at several major hubs), this can easily take 15–20 minutes on its own.

**Minutes 20–35: Any required security re-screening.** If you're staying entirely airside within the same airline's zone, this step may not apply at all. If it does — common at certain airports or with certain terminal layouts — budget 10–20 minutes depending on queue length, which is highly time-of-day dependent.

**Minutes 35–45: Buffer for the unexpected.** A slightly delayed inbound flight, a queue you didn't anticipate, a gate change announced after you'd already started walking — this is the margin that absorbs minor things going wrong.

**Minutes 45–60: Boarding.** Gates typically close 15–20 minutes before departure, which means you generally need to be at your new gate with time to spare, not arriving exactly as boarding ends.

Add this up, and a genuinely comfortable version of this sequence uses most or all of the 60 minutes even when everything goes right. There's very little room left over if any single step takes longer than the optimistic estimate — which means, in practice, that a meaningful share of 60-minute connections don't go entirely to plan, and some proportion of those result in a missed flight. This isn't a worst-case scenario being used to scare you off; it's simply what the maths above shows once you account for how often at least one step runs a little long.

## When 60 Minutes Is Realistically Enough

**Same terminal, adjacent or nearby gates.** If your arrival and departure gates are within easy walking distance and no security or immigration step is required, 60 minutes is workable, if not luxurious.

**Same airline or close alliance partner, single ticket.** This means your bags are checked through automatically and the airline is responsible for rebooking you if their own delay causes a problem — reducing both the practical steps required and the consequence if something does go wrong.

**A reliable inbound flight.** If your first flight has a strong on-time track record for that specific route and time of day, the odds of the buffer being eaten by a delay before you've even started your connection are lower.

**A compact, efficient airport.** Some airports are simply better designed for fast connections than others — shorter walking distances, well-signed routes, and less congestion all help a 60-minute connection actually work as intended.

---

## When 60 Minutes Is Not Enough

**Any terminal change.** As covered in more detail elsewhere, a terminal change alone can consume 20–45 minutes before you've even reached your new gate area — on top of everything else, this makes 60 minutes unrealistic in almost all cases.

**Any immigration or passport control step**, even for a brief transit. Queue times are the single most unpredictable variable in the entire process, and can range from a few minutes to well over half an hour depending on how many flights have landed around the same time.

**Two unrelated airlines on separate tickets (self-transfer).** Beyond the time pressure itself, a missed connection in this scenario means buying an entirely new ticket, since neither airline is obligated to help. The risk isn't just about timing — it's about what happens if the timing doesn't work out. See [Self-Transfer vs Protected Connection: The Risks Explained](/posts/self-transfer-vs-protected-connection) for more on this specific risk.

**A large, complex hub airport**, particularly ones where "same terminal" doesn't necessarily mean a short walk — several major airports split their largest terminals across multiple concourses or satellite piers, where an internal train or a lengthy walk is required even without technically changing terminals.

**An inbound flight with a history of running late.** If you can check your specific flight number's on-time performance and it regularly arrives behind schedule, treat the 60 minutes as effectively shorter than advertised.

## A Quick Self-Check

Before accepting a 60-minute connection, ask:

1. **Same terminal, or a change required?** Change required → 60 minutes is not enough.
2. **Any immigration step?** Yes → 60 minutes is not enough.
3. **Single ticket, or self-transfer?** Self-transfer → treat 60 minutes as high-risk regardless of the other factors.
4. **Does the airport have a reputation for long gate-to-gate distances?** Yes → lean toward assuming the higher end of walking time, not the optimistic estimate.

If your connection clears all four checks favourably, 60 minutes is a reasonable, if not generous, amount of time. If it fails even one, it's worth looking for an alternative connection with more buffer.

## What to Do If 60 Minutes Is Your Only Option

Sometimes a 60-minute connection is the only itinerary available, or the only one that fits your schedule. A few things reduce the risk:

**Travel with carry-on only if possible**, removing the need for your bag to make a tight connection along with you.

**Check in online and have your boarding pass ready before landing**, saving any time that would otherwise go toward printing or retrieving it at the gate.

**Move quickly and directly to your gate on arrival**, rather than stopping at shops or facilities — treat the connection as the priority until you've confirmed you have time to spare.

**Be mentally prepared to board among the last passengers and find overhead bin space already full.** If this happens, don't waste time searching multiple rows — flag it to cabin crew straightaway so your bag can be gate-checked or stowed elsewhere without delaying boarding further. Packing anything essential (medication, chargers, valuables) in a smaller personal item that fits under the seat in front avoids this being a problem even if your main carry-on does need to be gate-checked.

**Know your airline's app or contact details in case you do miss it**, so you can start the rebooking process as quickly as possible rather than losing time figuring out who to contact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will a booking system even let me book a 60-minute connection if it's not enough time?**
Booking systems generally only allow connections at or above the airport's published Minimum Connection Time, so a bookable 60-minute connection is, by definition, not against the rules — but the MCT is a legal minimum, not a guarantee of comfort, which is exactly the gap this article is addressing.

**Q: Is 60 minutes ever comfortable, or always just barely workable?**
At a small, efficient, single-terminal airport with adjacent gates and no immigration step, 60 minutes can feel genuinely comfortable rather than rushed. The variability is almost entirely about the specific airport and airline relationship, not a fixed property of "60 minutes" as a duration.

**Q: Does the type of aircraft affect how long deplaning takes?**
Yes, meaningfully — larger aircraft with more passengers and a single aisle take noticeably longer to deplane than smaller regional aircraft, and your specific seat location affects this further.

## Final Thoughts

Sixty minutes is not a safe margin — it's the minimum a booking system will allow, and the minute-by-minute breakdown above shows why that distinction matters. Even in the best-case scenario, there's little room for anything to go even slightly wrong, and enough regularly does go wrong (a late-running inbound flight, a longer walk than expected, an unanticipated queue) that a real proportion of 60-minute connections end in a missed flight. If a longer connection is available for a similar price, it's almost always worth taking it. If 60 minutes is genuinely your only option, go in with clear eyes about the risk, not an assumption that a bookable connection is automatically a safe one.
